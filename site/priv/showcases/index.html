Showcases

Games made with the library. In each game, each thing that moves is a boid: one Erlang process that senses, decides and acts. The server runs the game, and the page only draws it.

  • Rock PatrolKeys

    Fly a ship in a field of rocks. A queen makes drones, and the drones hunt you and shoot at you.

    Shows: a boid that a person controls, a rule that is a function, messages between boids, boids that start other boids, and a relation from each drone to its queen.

    Play

  • Long FlightCards

    Lead a flock to the exit of each land, past rocks and hawks. You never control a bird: after each land, you take a card and put the rules of the flock in an order.

    Shows: rules as data that change while the boids run, the priority order of the rules, and relations to elders.

    Play

  • Flock LabFor young programmers

    Puzzles for young programmers. Snap rule blocks together to tell the sheep what to do, then press Run and see if they reach the pen.

    Shows: a program as a list of rules, an order, numbers that change a behavior, "when" blocks, and the same program as Erlang and Elixir code.

    Play

  • Quiet HerdKeys

    Cross a field of grazing deer to reach the far side. A deer that sees you too near becomes afraid, and fear goes from deer to deer. Do not start a wave.

    Shows: a state of each boid (its fear) that changes its rules, and a behavior of the herd that no boid has: the wave of fear.

    Play

  • Living LandWatch and change

    Grazers eat, have young, and die. Their young get the weights of their rules with small changes. Hunters do the same. Change the land, and see what evolves.

    Shows: processes that are born and die all the time, rule weights as genes, and a world that a person changes while it runs.

    Play

One process for each boid

Each rock, drone, bird, deer and grazer is a gen_boid process with its own clock. It reads its neighbors from the world, and asks no other process.

The code of each game

Each game is one module of the site, in site/lib/boids_site/scenes. Read it to see how a game uses the library.

One world for each visitor

A game starts a world when you open its page, and stops it when you close the page. The site runs a small number of worlds at one time.

This site is the library itself: one Erlang VM in WebAssembly, made with beam.com. Each boid on a page is a process of that VM.
